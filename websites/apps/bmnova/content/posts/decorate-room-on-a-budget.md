## Quick Answer

To decorate a room on a budget, **decide the total before you look at anything**, keep the big pieces that still work, and put most of the money into the two or three changes that show the most: **lighting, textiles and one statement piece**. Preview the room with a photo-based design tool before you buy, so you only pay for things that work together.

---

## Step 1: Set a Number, Not a Range

A range ("somewhere around 300–600") always drifts to the top. Pick one number and write it down. Every decision after this is "does it fit the number?"

---

## Step 2: Audit What You Already Have

Walk the room and sort everything into three lists:

- **Keep:** works and fits the style you want
- **Rework:** fine shape, wrong look (paint it, re-cover it, move it to another room)
- **Replace:** broken, wrong size or blocking the layout

Most budget makeovers keep the sofa or bed and change everything around it.

---

## Step 3: Spend Where It Shows

A practical starting split for a small refresh:

| Category | Share of budget | Why |
|----------|-----------------|-----|
| Lighting (lamps, warm bulbs) | 20–25% | Changes the mood of the whole room at night |
| Textiles (rug, curtains, cushions, throw) | 30–35% | Adds colour and softness for little money |
| One statement piece (armchair, mirror, art) | 25–30% | Gives the room a focal point |
| Small decor and plants | 10–15% | Finishing layer; easiest to add later |

Paint is the cheapest big change if you rent with permission or own the place.

---

## Step 4: Preview Before You Buy

Most budget mistakes are pieces that look good alone and wrong together. Take a photo of your room and try a few styles on it first.

---

## Step 5: Buy in Order

1. Anything that changes the layout (rug, large furniture)
2. Lighting
3. Textiles
4. Decor and plants, last and slowly

Buying in this order stops small items from eating the budget meant for the pieces that matter.

## FAQ

**What is the cheapest way to make a room look expensive?**
Warm lighting in several spots, a properly sized rug and curtains hung high and wide. These change how the room feels more than any single decor item.

**How much does it cost to redecorate a room?**
It depends on the room and your city; a refresh that keeps the furniture can cost a fraction of a full makeover. Setting the number first and splitting it by category keeps it predictable.

**Can AI design my room from a photo?**
Yes. Photo-based AI interior design apps redesign your actual room in different styles; budget-aware ones like RoomPace also list real products that fit your number.

**Should I buy furniture or decor first?**
Furniture and the rug first, because they set the layout and the colour base. Decor goes last.
